# Phase 14.4 Plan 10: Condition in the bid premium decided, final report, phase exit Summary

**The sold-photo condition term was fitted on 186 flats and rejected by cross-validation (error 0,06359 without, 0,06362 with), so the bid premium keeps condition at 0 and says "skick ej mätt"; the final before/after report, the final replay and the full test suite (2 799 passed, tsc and lint clean) close phase 14.4.**

## What changed in the valuation

Three things changed, all of them measured on sold flats rather than assumed.

**The 20 % ceiling is gone.** Before this phase, the profit never counted more than 20 % of a flat's renovated value as gain, whatever the gap between the model's price and the expected purchase price. That was a stand-in for "cheap flats are usually cheap for reasons the model does not see". The model now sees the two reasons you named, so the stand-in was removed and every flat is credited with its full gap. In its place is a soft flag, "ovanligt långt under modellpris", that only explains and never changes a number.

**Avgift and association debt are now inside the price.** The model learned them from 1 200 recent sales (read through the free local browser, 0 USD Apify). Ten kronor more avgift per kvm and month lowers the price per kvm by about 0,64 %. A thousand kronor more association debt per kvm lowers it by about 0,24 %. Both are about three standard errors from zero, meaning the data is clear that the effect is real. Tomträtt (leasehold land) could not be estimated: every sold page that states a tenure says "Bostadsrätt", so there was nothing to compare, and the model records it as zero with that reason. The overall fit barely moved (R² 0,610 to 0,611; the typical miss, 0,115 in log units, is about 12 %). The value is in individual flats with unusual avgift or debt, not in a tighter average.

**Days on the market lower the expected overbid, not the resale value.** A flat that has sat for 60 days is expected to go for about 2,9 % over asking, against about 10,5 % for one sold after 7 days. That is a purchase-side effect only.

## Layout read

A separate picture read looks at the floor plan and photos and answers three questions per flat: which side the balcony is on, whether windows face two opposite sides (korsdrag, meaning air can pass through), and which side the bedroom is on. Anything under 0,6 confidence is treated as unknown.

On the live run of 75 flats (74 got a layout), the answers that passed the 0,6 gate were:

| Field | Usable answers | Of 75 |
|---|---|---|
| Balcony (street, courtyard or confidently none) | 41 | 55 % |
| Window sides (korsdrag known) | 40 | 53 % |
| Bedroom side | 13 | 17 % |

For 25 flats the sun score now uses the balcony side from the plan instead of the half-weight guess.

The accuracy check was done by eye on 12 plans, by me, on the Booli pictures only (I could not see the broker photos the read also saw). Whether a flat has a balcony at all matched Booli's own flag in 12 of 12. Balcony side was right in 7, wrong in 1, and could not be judged in 4 (the photos show neither a road nor a courtyard floor). Window sides were right in 8 and wrong in 3. Bedroom side could mostly not be judged, because the read said "okänd" at the gate, which is the honest answer. "Korsdrag nej" was let through for 4 flats and was right all 4 times. "Korsdrag ja" was let through for 3 flats and was wrong for 2 of them, at confidence 0,60 and 0,70 (interior glass between living room and sleeping alcove was probably taken for a window). Across the whole live run 12 flats got a "korsdrag ja" bonus, and if the rate in my sample holds, most of those 12 are wrong.

Folkungagatan 61 was misread as courtyard for both balcony and bedroom, at confidence 0,55. The "courtyard greenery" the model cited is most likely the artificial grass on the balcony floor. The 0,6 gate kept it out, so the table shows Korsdrag and Sovrum as "okänd" and the flat's taste score did not change.

Korsdrag and a quiet bedroom are taste, not market value. Each adds 1 % of resale to your taste score, inside the unchanged 5 % cap. No sold flat in our data has a floor plan, so the valuation cannot know what buyers pay for either. Nothing in the layout read changes what a flat is estimated to be worth.

## Bid premium

**Days: kept.** Coefficient -0,0351 per ln(1 + days), kept because the 5-fold error on held-out sales fell from 0,0736 to 0,0727 (837 sold flats). The form ln(1 + days) was fixed in advance and not searched over.

**Condition: tried and rejected.** This plan read the photos of 300 sold flats with the cheap Haiku model (26,12 kr, no failures). 113 reads came back under 0,6 confidence and were left out; they are not counted as "ordinary", because that would claim we knew. That left 187 usable labels, 186 of them with an asking price. The model called 8 of them dated (4,3 %), 39 ordinary (20,9 %) and 140 fresh (74,9 %).

On those 186 flats, adding a condition step (dated -1, ordinary 0, fresh +1) to the premium made the held-out error 0,06362 instead of 0,06359. That is not lower, so the rule rejects it. The estimated step is -0,0086, so a fresher flat went for 0,9 percentage points less over asking, which is the wrong direction for the hypothesis and about one standard error from zero. In plain terms the data shows no condition effect.

Two limits on that statement. First, 186 flats cannot see an effect under about 2 percentage points, and the research wanted about 200 to 220 usable rows. Second, and more important: the photos of sold flats almost never read as dated (8 of 187), so the comparison "dated against fresh" has almost no dated flats in it. What was really compared is ordinary against fresh. It is possible that dated flats do draw smaller overbids and this data cannot show it.

So the premium ignores condition, the table cell ends "skick ej mätt", and the run header reads "Budpremie skick: ej mätt (prior 0): korsvalidering förkastade termen (0,06359 -> 0,06362, skattning -0,0086). corr(skick, utgångspris/modell) = 0,16; en effekt under ≈ 2 pp kan inte ses med n = 186." The correlation of 0,16 means fresher flats are listed slightly higher against the model, so part of any condition effect may already sit in the asking price.

## Measured vs assumed

Measured, from stored data and re-runnable offline: the avgift and debt coefficients with their standard errors; the days term and its cross-validation numbers; the 17 flats the ceiling limited, in kronor per cause, with the recomputed gap matching the run record within 1 kr; how often the layout read answered and passed the gate; the by-eye agreement on 12 plans; the 300 sold-photo labels and the condition cross-validation.

Assumed or defined by a choice, not by data:
- The form ln(1 + days), the 0,6 confidence gate, and the 100-row minimum for the condition stage (the last one is my discretion: below it a fold holds out under 20 flats).
- The ±8 % limit on balcony and build-year effects. Two coefficients sit exactly on it (balcony +8,0 %, build year 1961 to 1990 -8,0 %), so the data wanted more than the limit allows.
- "Kronor explained by a fact" is defined as the change in the gap when that one fact is set to unknown. Effects multiply, so the parts do not add up alone; the remainder is printed as "Övrigt" (+249 437 kr over the 17 flats).
- Association debt is the latest annual report, not the figure at the sale date.
- A candidate's days on the market are "so far", while a sold flat's days are final. A fresh listing is compared with sold flats that include long ones, which makes the premium of a short listing somewhat too high. Documented in the model note, not corrected.
- The accuracy of the sold-photo labels themselves was never checked against a person. Only the model's own confidence was used.
- Taste weights (1 % each for korsdrag and a quiet bedroom) are your preference, not market value.

## Decisions for Daniel

1. **The ±8 % limit on balcony and build-year effects.** Two coefficients sit on it. Folkungagatan 61's move from rank 29 to 18 is mostly the balcony effect, so this limit decides where it and every other flat with a balcony lands. Lifting the limit would raise balcony flats further, and would also raise the share of the ranking that rests on a fitted number the library had been holding back. Nothing was changed.
2. **`RESIDUAL_FLAG_BASIS` in `src/lib/discovery/fit-score.ts`.** Today the flag compares the expected final price with the model and marks 10 of 77 flats. Switching to the asking price marks 26 of 77, because asking prices sit below sold prices by the expected overbid, so it fires on many ordinary flats. Flipping it changes only which rows carry the flag, never a profit figure.
3. **The 0,6 gate for window sides.** Both wrong "korsdrag ja" answers I found passed at 0,60 and 0,70, and the one right "ja" also sat at 0,70. Raising the bar to 0,75 would have removed all three, right and wrong. A wrong bonus is 1 % of resale, roughly 40 000 to 60 000 kr of taste score on these flats. The two misread korsdrag bonuses are in the live run's table until the gate or the read changes.
4. **Whether to try reading condition differently.** The 30 kr cap has 3,44 kr left. A different attempt (a graded scale, or only kitchen and bathroom pictures, or the 113 low-confidence flats) needs a decision to raise the cap, and it is only worth it if you want to know whether dated flats draw smaller overbids. Leaving it as is costs nothing: the premium says "skick ej mätt" and uses 0.
5. **Reading the rest of the sold pages (R-06).** Not needed. Both new coefficients are more than two standard errors from zero, so no more pages are read, and there is nothing for you to decide here.
