### (a) The enrichment and the price model

Booli stopped the one restart you allowed on 18 October after 64 pages, so the enrichment stands at 3 912 of 5 469 sold flats (72 %), which is where you paused it. It was 1 200 before this phase and 3 849 after the first run. Of the 3 912, 2 329 carry the association's debt per square metre. The second pass over associations never finished: 787 of 1 573 read. So the goal "enrich everything" is partly met, with the count above.

I refitted the price model on this larger set. Two numbers moved most:

- **Monthly fee.** 10 kr more per square metre and month lowers the price per square metre by 1,0 % now (95 % range roughly 0,8 to 1,2 %). It was 0,64 % with 1 200 flats and about twice as uncertain.
- **Association debt.** 1 000 kr more per square metre lowers the price by 0,25 %, same size as before but twice as certain.

The model has a second step where balcony and build year are priced, and it has a safety limit on how large those effects may be. At the old limit of plus or minus 8 %, two effects sat on the limit: a balcony (+8,3 %, uncertainty about 0,3 points) and a building from 1961 to 1990 (-11,2 %, uncertainty about 0,7 points). Before this phase both sat on the limit too, but nobody could see how far past it they wanted to go. I tried plus or minus 12 % once, as the rules allowed. On sales the model had not seen, the error was 0,10188 at 12 % against 0,10225 at 8 % (without the second step it is 0,11557). The rule said "widen if not worse", so the limit is now 12 % and nothing sits on it. The margin is thin, about 0,4 % of the error, and I ran no significance test. If you prefer the tighter limit, it is a one-file swap and only flats built 1961 to 1990 change.

### (b) What moved, Folkungagatan 61 first

Folkungagatan 61 goes from place 17 to place 16 in the replay of the 13 October list. Expected resale price falls from 5 503 113 to 5 288 313 kr (down 214 800 kr) and profit from 1 123 047 to 938 661 kr (down 184 386 kr). The refit and the wider limit added only 4 934 kr to its resale value. Almost all of the fall comes from a bug I fixed: the model counted the average balcony bonus twice for any flat whose balcony status was known, so every resale value was 4,0 % too high. That fix lowered all 77 flats by the same 4,0 % and changed the place of 35 of them. Places 1 to 6 keep their order but their profits fall 14 to 18 %. The biggest drops were Tideliusgatan 55 (58 to 68), Ringvägen 124 (61 to 69), Reimersholmsgatan 39 (24 to 30) and Idungatan 5 (18 to 23). John Bergs Plan 4 (63 to 57) and Rålambsvägen 70 (68 to 63) rose.

The consequence for how you read the weekly table: every profit is lower than before this change, so fewer flats clear a fixed profit threshold. Do not compare tables from before and after. Folkungagatan 61 is not for sale in today's run ("finns inte i körningen"), so today's table has no place for it.

### (c) Today's run: ceiling height and the real-text check

- **Ceiling height.** 1 of 33 read descriptions states a height: 2,75 m, below the 3,0 m where the bonus starts. One more says "högt i tak" with no number, which earns nothing by design. So no flat got a ceiling bonus, and none reached the 5 % taste cap because of it. 3 % of 33 is a very rough number; with a sample that small and cut short, the honest reading is "brokers rarely write it".
- **Why the price model cannot use ceiling height.** Sold pages on Booli carry no broker text, so 0 of the 3 912 enriched sold flats have a stated height. The rule needed 300. The height shows in the table and mail column and in the taste term only.
- **Real broker text (the check deferred from the bygglov/stambyte phase).** I read the masked sentences the audit printed for 33 texts (40 were wanted, 33 were available). In none of them did a broker say where the area figure comes from. Nine texts printed a sentence with a look-alike word, eight of them standard wording on the seller's liability, and the reader correctly gave "okänd" for all of them. So the area reader has 0 hits and 0 seen misses. That does not prove the reader works. It proves that brokers almost never write the thing it looks for, so the Area column stays "okänd" for every flat. The audit only prints sentences containing its keywords, so a statement worded differently would not show up.
- **Stambyte reader.** The audit does not print stambyte sentences, so I could not judge it against real text. Counts only: 2 of 33 texts gave a year (2024 and 2017); 12 of the 58 rows show some stambyte value, from text or from the city's case list.

**Switch verdict: off.** The rule needed one variant that passes all three things. Balcony side passes in three variants, but korsdrag cannot reach the bar: only 4 of the 60 flats truly have windows on two opposite sides, so even a perfect reader gives at most 3 believed "yes" answers on the development half, and 5 are needed to pick a cutoff. Bedroom side has 9 flats where a judge could tell, and 12 are needed. Nothing in the weekly mail changes: Korsdrag, Sovrum and Sol cells from the floor plan stay "okänd", and the weekly job keeps reading and saving layouts so the measurement can go on.

Two ways to move korsdrag, neither taken. First, add more flats with windows on two opposite sides to the set (I estimate 8 to 10 in total, not measured), lock it again and re-read. Second, count the 12-flat minimum over flats that truly have the feature, which makes korsdrag "not measurable" like the bedroom side. Changing the measure should be decided before anyone sees which variant would then pass. An idea I have not tested: let Jev (0,008 kr) read every flat first, and send only the flats where Jev is unsure to Opus (0,51 kr). That could cut the cost of an Opus-level read, but the numbers above are too small to say it would hold the accuracy.

Caveats: the 60 flats were picked because a judge could tell the answer, not at random, so real accuracy on ordinary listings is probably lower. The judge was a Claude model working from pictures. Every round read Booli pictures only. Five variants were tested on the same 28 held-back flats, which makes a lucky result more likely than for a single variant.
