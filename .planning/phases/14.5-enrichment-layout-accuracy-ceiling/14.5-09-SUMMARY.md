## What it means

The question was whether any way of reading the floor plan and photos gets balcony side, korsdrag (windows on two opposite sides, giving a cross-draught) and bedroom side right at least 75 % of the time when it commits to an answer. After five variants the answer is no, but for a different reason per field:

- **Balcony side** is solved by three variants. Opus (c) was right on all 6 holdout answers it committed to, the two-step read on Sonnet (d) on all 5, Jev (e) on 4 of 5. The caveat is the sample: 5 or 6 answers on the holdout half, so even a perfect score has a lower bound of 57 to 61 %, and Jev's 4 of 5 has a lower bound of 38 %. Five variants were tried against the same holdout, which raises the chance that one looks good by luck. The pooled numbers (dev and holdout together, 11 to 13 answers) are the steadier evidence and tell the same story for c, d and e.
- **Korsdrag** passes nowhere, and the reason is counting, not reading quality. The truth set has 4 flats with windows on two opposite sides (3 on dev, 1 on holdout). The rule needs at least 5 committed "ja" answers on dev to pick a gate. A reader that says "ja" only where it is true can give at most 3. Opus got closest: it said "ja" on 4 dev flats and was right on 3 (75 %, one answer short of the 5 needed), and its raw korsdrag accuracy was 57 of 58. The other variants either over-called "ja" (a: 7 calls, 2 right; b: 19 calls, 3 right) or said it too rarely. I did not lower the bar.
- **Bedroom side** has 9 decided truth rows and 12 are needed, so it is "ej mätbart" for every variant. Raw, Opus read it right 8 of 8 and the two-step read 6 of 6, which is encouraging and unprovable on this set.

If korsdrag is ever to be switched on, the truth set needs more flats that really have two opposite window sides (the 12-row minimum should arguably count those, not all 59 decided flats). That is a decision for Daniel, not something this plan could settle (see `D4` in the coverage list).
